## Accounting support built for churches and religious organizations

Church finances carry a weight most small businesses never touch. Fund accounting separates restricted gifts from general operating dollars. Donor reporting has to satisfy a congregation, a board, and sometimes a denominational office all at once. Clergy compensation follows its own tax rules, and getting it wrong creates real liability for the church.

Accord Advisors built its [outsourced accounting](/services/outsourced-accounting) model around exactly this kind of complexity, drawing on close to 40 years of CPA experience serving churches and organizations across Bloomington, Avon, Indianapolis, and throughout Indiana. Our team works directly with church boards, office administrators, and church leaders statewide to keep the books accurate without adding another task to a pastor's plate. The goal is simple: leadership sees clear numbers, and staff gets back to ministry instead of spreadsheets.

## Payroll processing for pastors and church staff

Pastor payroll is not standard payroll. Housing allowances have to be calculated and documented correctly, or the exemption doesn't hold up if the IRS asks questions later. Clergy hold dual tax status, treated as employees for income tax purposes but self-employed for Social Security and Medicare, which changes how withholding and quarterly estimates work. Layer in part-time staff, nursery workers, and volunteer stipends, and the payroll picture gets complicated fast.

Accord Advisors runs [payroll processing](/services/payroll-services) for churches across Bloomington, Avon, Indianapolis, and throughout Indiana with the housing allowance and dual-status rules built into every pay run. Staff get paid accurately and on time. Withholding gets calculated correctly the first time, not corrected after a W-2 goes out wrong. Boards get a payroll process they don't have to double-check, because it was set up right from the start by a CPA who has done this before.

## Staying compliant with nonprofit and religious organization rules

Religious organizations get some tax exemptions most businesses don't, but exemption doesn't mean no paperwork. Payroll tax filings still go out quarterly. W-2s and 1099s still have to be issued correctly and on time, including for contract musicians, guest speakers, and part-time custodial staff. Rules around unrelated business income, love offerings, and benevolence payments carry their own reporting requirements, separate from a typical nonprofit.

Accord Advisors keeps churches current on the filings that matter most:

- Quarterly and annual payroll tax filings (941s, 940s, state withholding)
- W-2 and 1099 preparation and filing for staff and contractors
- Housing allowance documentation that holds up under review
- Recordkeeping for love offerings, benevolence funds, and restricted gifts
- Coordination with the board on IRS rules specific to religious nonprofits

Staying ahead of these deadlines means the church is audit-ready every month of the year, not just scrambling each January. For a related look at protecting church funds, see this guide on [fraud prevention strategies for nonprofit organizations](/fraud-prevention-strategies-for-nonprofit-organizations).

## Clear monthly financial reporting for church leadership

Boards and finance committees want one thing above all: a clear answer to how the church is doing financially, delivered on a schedule they can count on. Guesswork and shoebox reporting don't cut it when elders are making decisions about staffing or building projects.

Accord Advisors delivers financial reports and dashboards through a secure online platform, so the treasurer, board, and pastor can see current numbers without waiting on a spreadsheet emailed after the fact. Reports break down income by fund, track restricted versus unrestricted gifts, and flag anything that needs attention before it grows into a bigger issue. Everything is available on demand, any month, not just at year-end review. That kind of visibility lets leadership make decisions based on where the church actually stands financially, not where it stood three months ago.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Staffing and compliance support for churches

**Q: Does Accord Advisors handle pastor housing allowance calculations?**
A: Yes. Accord Advisors calculates and documents housing allowances as part of every payroll run, so the exemption holds up if the IRS ever asks questions. This is built into the payroll process for churches in Bloomington and Indianapolis, not handled as a separate add-on.

**Q: How does clergy dual tax status affect church payroll?**
A: Pastors are treated as employees for income tax but self-employed for Social Security and Medicare. That changes how withholding and quarterly estimates are calculated. Accord Advisors sets up payroll to reflect dual status correctly from the start, avoiding corrections after W-2s go out.

**Q: What compliance filings does a church need each year?**
A: Churches still file quarterly and annual payroll tax forms, issue W-2s and 1099s for staff and contractors, and document restricted gifts and love offerings. Accord Advisors manages these filings year-round so the church stays audit-ready, not scrambling each January.

**Q: Does Accord Advisors charge hourly for church accounting services?**
