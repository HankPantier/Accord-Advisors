## Why monthly numbers matter more than year-end reports

Most optometry practice owners find out how their practice actually performed sometime around April, when the tax return lands on their desk. By then the slow month in frames sales, the spike in supply costs, or the dip in exam volume is old news. You can't fix a problem you found out about four months late.

A practice generating $1.2 million a year moves through roughly $100,000 in transactions every month. That's a lot of decisions happening without a rearview mirror, let alone a dashboard. Owners in Bloomington and Indianapolis tell us the same thing: they want to know how last month went while there's still time to adjust staffing, ordering, or pricing before the next month repeats the mistake.

Monthly financial reporting turns guesswork into a habit of informed decisions. That's the shift this page is about.

## Financial metrics optometry owners should monitor monthly

A year-end P&L tells you what happened. Monthly metrics tell you what's happening now, while you can still do something about it. These are the numbers we build into every optometry client's monthly reporting package:

- **Revenue per patient encounter**, tracks whether your average ticket is growing, shrinking, or getting propped up by a handful of high-dollar frame sales
- **Cost of goods on frames and contacts**, flags supplier price creep or inventory waste before it erodes your margin
- **Overhead ratio**, measures staffing, rent, and lab costs against revenue so you know if growth is actually profitable growth
- **Cash flow trends**, shows whether receivables, insurance reimbursements, and payment timing are keeping the practice liquid month to month

These aren't abstract accounting concepts. They're the inputs to real decisions: whether to add a doctor day, renegotiate a lab contract, or hold off on that new piece of equipment. Outsourced accounting only earns its keep if it hands you numbers you can act on the same week you get them, not the same year.

## Non-financial data that shapes smarter decisions

Financial statements only tell half the story. A practice can look profitable on paper and still be losing ground on patient retention or exam capacity. Practice managers need operational data sitting right next to the numbers, not in a separate binder.

Patient recall rates show whether your reminder system is actually bringing people back for annual exams, which drives both revenue and continuity of care. Exam-to-sales conversion tells you if doctors are recommending appropriate eyewear and whether opticians are closing on those recommendations. Staff productivity, measured in exams per provider hour or sales per optician, points to scheduling and staffing decisions long before payroll costs show up as a problem in the financials.

We build these into the same monthly dashboard as the accounting metrics, because an optometry practice manager shouldn't have to reconcile two different systems to understand one practice.
