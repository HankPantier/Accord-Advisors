## A firm built on decades of local accounting expertise

Nearly 40 years is a long time to spend in one profession, in one region. RootAdvisors has been doing the books, filing the returns, and answering the tough questions for Bloomington and Indianapolis business owners since long before cloud accounting existed. Jared Hammack, CPA, leads a firm that grew up alongside the businesses it serves, not one that parachuted in from a national office to sell a template.

That history matters. National firms rotate staff, standardize advice, and bill by the hour for every phone call. A firm rooted in central Indiana for four decades knows which local banks move fastest on a loan, which industries are hiring, and which tax rules actually apply to a dentist in Bloomington versus a service contractor in Indianapolis. Clients don't get a stranger every renewal season. They get the same advisor, year after year, who already knows their business before the meeting starts.

## Deep experience with optometry and dental practices

Optometry and dental practices run on numbers most CPAs never learn: capitation rates, insurance panel mixes, associate buy-in structures, and equipment depreciation schedules that stretch a decade or more. RootAdvisors has worked through those numbers with practice owners for close to 40 years, long enough to spot a staffing cost creeping out of line before it becomes a crisis.

Many optometry clients arrive through [Acquios](https://acquios.com/), the industry referral network connecting practice managers with accountants who already speak the language of OD practices. That's not a coincidence. Understanding [practice management metrics](/industries/optometry/practice-management-metrics), from patient volume to exam-chair utilization, takes repetition, not a crash course. [Dentists](/industries/dentists) face a parallel set of challenges: hygienist payroll, lab fees, and multi-location scheduling. Decades in both fields means fewer surprises and faster answers when a practice owner calls with a question that can't wait for a scheduled meeting.

## Trusted by medical professionals and healthcare providers

Healthcare reimbursement does not sit still. Payer mixes shift, coding rules change, and cash flow can swing hard between a slow claims month and a catch-up payment. Physicians, physician assistants, and other [medical professionals](/industries/medical-professionals) who bring their books to RootAdvisors get an advisor who has watched these patterns play out across nearly four decades of practices in Bloomington and Indianapolis.

That experience shows up in specifics: knowing which [reimbursement challenges](/industries/medical-professionals/reimbursement-challenges) tend to hit primary care differently than a specialty practice, or recognizing when a slow quarter is seasonal rather than a warning sign. Jared Hammack, CPA, and the RootAdvisors team use that pattern recognition to help healthcare providers plan cash flow around the realities of medical billing, not a generic small-business model.

## Experience supporting service businesses through every stage

Service businesses rarely stay the same shape for long. A two-person shop becomes a ten-person operation, then starts thinking about fleet vehicles, equipment financing, or a succession plan for the owner who built it. RootAdvisors has guided [service-based clients](/industries/service-businesses) across Bloomington and Indianapolis through each of those stages, from the first year of incorporation through the day an owner hands off the business.

That range of experience means startup questions get the same seasoned attention as a succession plan. An owner adding a second truck or a fleet of vans benefits from [fleet and equipment planning](/industries/service-businesses/fleet-and-equipment-planning) grounded in real depreciation schedules, not guesswork. An owner nearing retirement gets a team that has already walked other clients through a [sale or an internal transition](/services/succession-planning), because that work has been part of the practice for years.

## A long history of guiding churches and religious organizations

Church finances follow their own rules: designated funds, stewardship reporting to a congregation instead of shareholders, staffing structures that mix pastoral roles with lay employees, and compliance requirements unique to religious nonprofits. RootAdvisors has worked with [churches and religious organizations](/industries/churches) in and around Bloomington long enough to know these are not edge cases. They are a core part of the practice.

That means fewer generic nonprofit templates and more direct answers about [staffing and compliance](/industries/churches/staffing-and-compliance) for an administrator trying to keep payroll correct for both W-2 staff and clergy with special tax treatment. It also means practical guidance on [fraud prevention](/fraud-prevention-strategies-for-nonprofit-organizations), since limited financial oversight is a real risk for many congregations. Decades in this niche translate into faster, more specific answers than a firm encountering church accounting for the first time.
