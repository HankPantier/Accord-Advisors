## See your practice or business performance at a glance

Most business owners can tell you what happened last quarter. Fewer can tell you what happened last month, and almost none can tell you what's happening right now. That gap is exactly what keeps owners up at night: was collections down because of a slow week, or something worse? Did payroll costs creep up again?

The insights gallery below shows the kind of reporting Accord Advisors clients see every month, not once a year at tax time. Led by Jared Hammack, CPA, our team has spent nearly 40 years building financial reporting specifically for healthcare providers, and we've carried that same discipline into service businesses and churches across Bloomington and Indianapolis. These aren't generic templates. Each dashboard is built around the numbers that actually drive decisions in your industry.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: optometry-practice-dashboard-review.jpg | alt: "Optometry practice manager reviewing production and collections dashboard on a laptop" | query: "optometrist reviewing financial dashboard laptop" -->
## Dashboards built for optometry, dental, and medical practices

An optometry practice manager doesn't need a generic profit and loss statement. They need to know how exam volume compares to last month, how collections track against production, and whether a particular provider's schedule is running efficiently. The same holds true for dental and medical practices, where reimbursement timing and payer mix can swing cash flow from one month to the next.

Our healthcare dashboards pull that data into a single view: production by provider, collection rates, patient-visit trends, and accounts receivable aging. Practice owners referred through networks like [Acquios](https://acquios.com/) see this reporting from month one. If you manage or own an [optometry practice](/industries/optometry), a [dental office](/industries/dentists), or another [medical practice](/industries/medical-professionals), the metrics you review each month should match the ones already tracked on our [practice management metrics](/industries/optometry/practice-management-metrics) page.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-left | image: church-financial-report-meeting.jpg | alt: "Church administrator and bookkeeper reviewing giving and expense report together" | query: "nonprofit administrator reviewing financial report" -->
## Insights for service businesses and churches

A landscaping company and a downtown church don't share a chart of accounts, but they share the same underlying question: are we spending in line with what's coming in? Service businesses need job costing, labor utilization, and margin-by-project data. Churches need giving trends, fund balances, and compliance-ready reports for the board or elders.

The same secure platform delivers both. A [service business](/industries/service-businesses) owner can log in and see fleet costs against revenue by job. A [church](/industries/churches) treasurer can pull a giving trend report before a finance committee meeting without waiting on a bookkeeper to compile it manually. Different data, same reliable access, built around what each organization actually needs to track.

## Real-time access through a secure online platform

Firms that bill by the hour have little incentive to hand you real-time numbers. Every login, every question, every extra report can turn into a line item. That model works against the very thing you need most: quick, current answers.

Accord Advisors works differently. Clients pay a fixed monthly fee and get ongoing access to a secure online platform, not a static PDF emailed once a quarter. Log in anytime to see current balances, receivables, and the same dashboards your accountant is reviewing. When a bank asks for updated financials or you're deciding whether to make a hire, you're not waiting on a callback. The numbers are already there, current as of your last close.

## Turning numbers into decisions for growing businesses

A dashboard only matters if it changes what you do next. A startup optometry practice watching patient-visit trends can decide whether it's time to add a second provider. A service business tracking equipment costs against project margins can decide whether that next truck purchase pencils out. A practice nearing succession can use years of clean, consistent reporting to support a stronger valuation when it's time to sell.

That's the role of outsourced accounting done right: not just closing the books, but giving owners the information to act on. Our [outsourced accounting](/services/outsourced-accounting) engagements are built around this idea from day one, and our [business foundation services](/services/business-foundation-services) make sure the underlying bookkeeping is solid enough to support the dashboard on top of it. Whether you're two years into a practice or twenty, the reporting should scale with you.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Insights gallery

**Q: What is the insights gallery on the Accord Advisors website?**
A: It's a showcase of sample financial dashboards Accord Advisors builds for clients each month, covering optometry, dental, medical, service business, and church organizations. **Q: How often are dashboards updated?**
A: Dashboards are part of the monthly outsourced accounting process and reflect current data through your secure online platform login, not just a once-a-year tax-time snapshot. Clients can log in between monthly closes to check current balances and receivables.

**Q: Do churches and service businesses get the same reporting as medical practices?**
A: The platform is the same, but the metrics differ. Medical and optometry clients see production and collections data, while service businesses see job costing and equipment costs, and churches see giving trends and fund balances.

**Q: Is there an extra cost to access the online dashboard?**
A: No. Dashboard access is included in Accord Advisors' fixed monthly fee for outsourced accounting clients. There's no hourly billing for logging in or reviewing your numbers, unlike firms that charge per request.
