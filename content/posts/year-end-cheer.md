## Close out your year with confidence

Every December, the same calls come in: a practice manager realizes the books haven't been reconciled since August, or a business owner opens a drawer full of receipts three weeks before their CPA needs numbers. Year end doesn't have to feel like a scramble.

Accord Advisors handles the cleanup, the categorization, and the tax planning conversations before they become a December fire drill. Bloomington and Indianapolis clients work with a fixed monthly fee, a secure online platform, and a team that already knows their books, so nothing gets rushed and nothing gets missed. If you're heading into year end wondering what shape your financials are actually in, that's exactly the conversation to have now, not in January.

## What year end accounting cleanup includes

Year end cleanup is more than closing a spreadsheet. It's the difference between financial statements your lender or tax preparer can actually rely on and a set of books that raise more questions than they answer. Accord Advisors treats December and January as a checklist, not a guessing game:

- Reconciling every bank, credit card, and loan account against monthly statements
- Reviewing the general ledger for miscategorized transactions before they distort your financials
- Cleaning up chart-of-accounts inconsistencies that build up over a busy year
- Preparing accurate year-end financial statements for your CPA, lender, or board
- Confirming payroll totals, contractor payments, and asset records tie out correctly

This is the same work an in-house bookkeeper or controller would handle, delivered through Accord Advisors' outsourced accounting service at a fixed monthly fee instead of hourly billing. You get clean books without adding a salary to your overhead.

## Year end tax planning before the deadline crunch

Tax planning that starts in April, when returns are due, is really just tax filing. Real planning happens in October, November, and December, while there's still time to act on what the numbers show. Jared Hammack, CPA, leads these conversations with clients throughout the fourth quarter: reviewing estimated tax payments, flagging retirement plan contribution deadlines, and identifying deductions that get missed when cleanup happens after the fact.

This is where Accord Advisors' Business Foundation Services connect directly to the accounting work. Clean, reconciled books make tax planning conversations faster and more accurate, because there's no guessing at what the numbers actually say. Clients get a specific list of moves to consider before December 31, not a generic year-end checklist pulled from a newsletter. For a practice owner or business owner in Bloomington or Indianapolis, that's the difference between a surprise tax bill and a plan you saw coming.

## Support built for optometrists, dentists, and medical professionals

Healthcare and optometry practices carry year-end complications that a general small business doesn't face: insurance reimbursement timing, equipment depreciation schedules, and staffing costs that shift with patient volume. Accord Advisors has served healthcare providers for nearly 40 years, which means the year-end review isn't a generic template. It's built around how optometry and medical practices actually operate.

For practices referred through Acquios, that year-end review often includes benchmarking against practice management metrics that matter to buyers, lenders, and management companies evaluating performance. Dentists and medical professionals weighing a partnership change, a lender conversation, or a practice sale need financials that hold up under scrutiny, not just a P&L that balances. Clean, well-documented books at year end make every one of those conversations shorter and less stressful.

## Year end guidance for service businesses and churches

Service businesses and churches close out the year with their own specific requirements. Service businesses need contractor 1099s issued correctly and on time, clean job-cost records, and financials that support decisions about equipment purchases or staffing for the year ahead. Miss a 1099 deadline and it's a penalty; miss the underlying cost data and it's a worse decision made with bad information.

Churches face a different set of expectations. Board members and congregations want clear, accurate year-end statements. Donor contribution statements need to go out correctly and on time to keep giving records straight for members' own tax filing. Accord Advisors builds these deliverables into the year-end process for religious organizations across Bloomington and Indianapolis, so the finance committee walks into January with answers instead of open questions.
