## Why traditional budgets break down when prices keep rising

Most budgets start with last year's numbers and add a percentage for inflation. That worked fine when prices moved two percent a year. It stops working when a bag of exam-room supplies costs 18% more than it did in 2021, or when a hygienist's starting wage has climbed four dollars an hour since your last budget cycle.

Practice owners and service business owners across Bloomington and Indianapolis are finding that last year's budget, adjusted upward, no longer reflects what things actually cost today. Rent renewals, payroll, insurance premiums, and vendor contracts have all moved independently of each other, and a flat percentage bump doesn't capture any of it.

Zero-based budgeting throws out the assumption that last year's numbers are a fair starting point. Every dollar gets justified from zero, every month, based on what your business actually needs right now. For a CPA firm working with optometry practices, dental offices, and service businesses managing rising costs on multiple fronts, that distinction matters.

## How zero based budgeting works for your business or household

Zero-based budgeting flips the traditional approach. Instead of starting with what you spent last year and adjusting it up or down, you start every month at zero and build the budget from scratch, dollar by dollar, based on what the business actually needs during that period.

For a practice owner, that means asking real questions each month. Does the office need a new round of exam supplies, or is inventory already stocked? Is a locum covering vacation time this month, adding a temporary labor cost? Is there a seasonal marketing push planned? Every expense has to earn its place in the budget instead of coasting in because it was there last year.

The same logic applies at home. Rather than assuming last year's grocery or utility number still holds, every dollar of income gets assigned a specific job before the month begins: rent, savings, debt payoff, discretionary spending.

The result is a budget that reflects what's actually happening in a business or household right now, not what happened twelve months ago when supply costs and wages looked different.

## Common mistakes local business owners make when they try this alone

Zero-based budgeting sounds simple in theory: start at zero, justify every dollar. In practice, business owners who try it without clean books underneath run into the same problems.

- Building the budget on numbers that are already out of date, because bookkeeping is two or three months behind
- Mixing personal and business expenses, which makes it impossible to tell what a category actually costs
- Skipping the monthly rebuild after the first cycle, so the zero-based budget quietly turns back into last year's budget
- Working without a consistent chart of accounts, so this month's numbers can't be compared to last month's
- Treating it as a once-a-year project instead of a recurring, month-by-month discipline

Every one of these mistakes traces back to the same root cause: the underlying financials weren't accurate or current enough to build a real budget on top of them.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Zero based budgeting a smarter way to manage your money during persistent inflation

**Q: What is zero-based budgeting?**
A: Zero-based budgeting means building a budget from zero every period instead of adjusting last year's numbers. Every expense, from supplies to payroll, has to be justified against current need each month. It gives a more accurate picture during periods when costs like wages and materials are rising at different rates.

**Q: How is zero-based budgeting different from a traditional budget?**
A: A traditional budget starts with last year's figures and adjusts by a percentage. Zero-based budgeting starts at zero each month and rebuilds every line item from scratch based on current needs. That makes it more responsive when costs like insurance, supplies, or wages move at uneven rates.

**Q: Can a small optometry or dental practice realistically use zero-based budgeting?**
A: Yes, but it depends on having accurate, current books to build from. Practices with a solid monthly close process can rebuild their budget each month without much extra effort, since the underlying numbers are already reliable and up to date.
