## Why labor shortages are pushing employers toward upskilling

Finding qualified staff in Bloomington and across the Indianapolis area has gotten harder, not easier. Optometry practices need trained technicians who can run new diagnostic equipment. Dental offices need hygienists and front-desk staff who understand insurance billing. Medical practices, service businesses, and churches are all competing for the same shrinking pool of skilled workers.

Hiring from outside costs more than most owners expect once recruiting fees, onboarding time, and lost productivity get added up. Training the staff already on your payroll is often the more affordable path forward, and it carries a benefit many employers overlook: money spent on employee education and skills training can reduce your tax bill. Understanding how those training costs flow through your books is where the real savings show up.

<!-- block: content-prose -->
## How workforce training expenses affect your business taxes

The federal tax code generally treats employee training as an ordinary and necessary business expense, the same category as rent or office supplies. Continuing education for licensed staff, software and equipment training, safety certifications, and skills courses tied to an employee's current position typically qualify for a deduction in the year you pay for them.

The line gets blurrier when training prepares an employee for a different job or profession rather than improving performance in the one they already hold. That distinction matters more than most business owners realize, and getting it wrong can mean missing a deduction you're entitled to or claiming one you shouldn't.

This is general information, not tax advice for your specific situation. Every practice and business has different facts, and the right treatment depends on what the training covers, who receives it, and how it's documented. That's a conversation worth having before tax season, not during it.

## Indiana resources and credits that support employee development

Indiana offers more support for workforce training than many employers realize. The Indiana Department of Workforce Development runs programs and grant opportunities designed to help employers train new and existing staff, and state lawmakers have periodically discussed expanding tax incentives tied to workforce credentialing.

Knowing these programs exist is one thing. Applying them correctly to your specific practice or business, and making sure the numbers land where they should on your tax return, is another. We help Bloomington and Indianapolis-area business owners figure out which state programs actually apply to their situation, then make sure the accounting reflects it accurately so nothing gets left on the table at filing time.

## What this means for optometry, dental, and medical practices

Clinical staffing is its own puzzle. [Optometry](/industries/optometry) practices need opticians and technicians who can handle new equipment and evolving insurance requirements. [Dental](/industries/dentists) offices depend on hygienists and assistants trained on current sterilization and billing standards. [Medical](/industries/medical-professionals) practices face similar pressure with clinical staff and front-desk teams who manage scheduling, coding, and patient intake.

Training an existing employee to take on a higher-skill role, cross-training front-desk staff on billing software, or funding a technician's certification renewal are all costs that typically qualify for deduction, provided they're documented and tied to the employee's current position. Practices that track these expenses correctly get two wins: a more capable team and a cleaner tax return.

We work with optometry, dental, and medical practices across Bloomington and Indianapolis to make sure training costs are categorized correctly from the start, not reconstructed at year-end.

## What this means for service businesses and churches

Service businesses face a different version of the same problem: skilled trades and technical roles that take months to fill from outside. Training current employees on new equipment, safety protocols, or licensing requirements is often faster and cheaper than hiring, and the costs are typically deductible when properly documented.

Churches carry an added layer. Paid staff training, such as bookkeeping software instruction for an office administrator or safety training for facilities staff, is generally treated like any other business expense. Volunteer training is different and usually isn't deductible the same way, which makes clear recordkeeping between paid and volunteer roles essential.

Getting the distinction right protects a church's tax-exempt status and keeps the books audit-ready. Learn more about staffing considerations for [churches](/industries/churches) or explore support for [service businesses](/industries/service-businesses).

## Frequently Asked Questions About Upskilling your workforce a smart strategy for overcoming labor market shortages

**Q: Are employee training costs tax deductible for Indiana businesses?**
A: In most cases, yes. Training that maintains or improves an employee's skills in their current role is generally treated as an ordinary business expense and deducted in the year it's paid. Whether a specific course or certification qualifies depends on your facts, which is why documentation matters.

**Q: What Indiana programs help employers pay for workforce training?**
A: The Indiana Department of Workforce Development offers grant programs and resources aimed at helping employers train new and current staff. Availability and eligibility change over time, so confirming current programs before you budget for training is worth the extra step.

**Q: How does outsourced accounting help track training expense deductions?**
A: Outsourced accounting through RootAdvisors codes training costs correctly each month instead of sorting through receipts at tax time. That means clean monthly reports and a return built on a full year of accurate records rather than a last-minute reconstruction.

**Q: Can churches deduct training costs for paid staff?**
A: Generally, yes, training for paid employees is treated like any other legitimate business expense. Volunteer training typically isn't deductible the same way, so separating paid staff costs from volunteer-related spending in your books is essential for accurate reporting.
