## Setbacks don't have to define your business

A lost contract. A rough year in patient volume. A location that never found its footing. Whatever brought you here, the number that matters most right now isn't the one on last year's tax return, it's the one that tells you what to do next.

Business owners in Bloomington and Indianapolis call Accord Advisors after a setback, not before one, and that's fine. Jared Hammack, CPA, has built a practice around steady, judgment-free numbers rather than lectures. Recovery starts with clarity, not blame. If your business needs a plan more than a pep talk, this is where that plan begins.

## Understanding what went wrong with clear financial data

Most owners can name the symptom of a bad year: cash was always tight, payroll felt like a monthly emergency, or revenue looked fine but the bank account never agreed. Naming the cause is harder without current books.

Outsourced accounting through Accord Advisors replaces guesswork with a monthly dashboard that shows exactly where cash flow broke down, whether billing delays, overhead creep, or a pricing problem drained the margin. Clients consistently say they want to know how the business performed each month and lack the data to see it clearly. That gap gets closed first, before any rebuilding decisions get made.

Once the numbers are current and organized on a secure online platform, patterns show up fast. A slow-paying payer mix. An overstaffed shift. A vendor contract that quietly crept up. Clear data turns a vague sense that something went wrong into a specific, fixable list.

## Rebuilding your business foundation the right way

A setback is often the first honest look a business gets at its own foundation. Bookkeeping that fell behind, an entity structure that never matched the business's growth, payroll that got messy under pressure: these are fixable, and fixing them now prevents a repeat.

Business Foundation Services from Accord Advisors address the structural issues, not just the symptoms that followed:

- Entity structure review to confirm S-corp, LLC, or partnership status still fits the business
- Bookkeeping system rebuild with monthly reconciliations, not year-end scrambles
- Payroll cleanup, including back filings and worker classification checks
- Chart of accounts redesign so reports actually answer the owner's questions
- Documented processes that survive staff turnover or an owner's absence

A rebuild done at the foundation level, rather than a patch on top of old problems, is what keeps the next setback from looking exactly like this one.

## Tax planning after a loss or restructuring

A hard year on the P&L can still create real tax value. Net operating losses carried forward, asset sales tied to a closed location, or a restructuring that changes entity type all carry tax consequences that reward planning and punish neglect.

Coordinating business and personal tax strategy matters more during a comeback than during steady years. An owner who sold equipment at a loss, changed entity structure, or drew down personal savings to keep payroll running needs those moves reflected correctly on both returns, not treated as separate problems handled by separate preparers.

Accord Advisors' Personal Income Tax and business tax planning work together for exactly this reason. Loss carryforwards get tracked and applied at the right time. Restructuring gets modeled before it happens, not reported after the fact. A comeback year is often the most tax-efficient year a business will have, if the paperwork and the strategy line up.
