## Why the new year is a natural checkpoint for your finances

January gives you something the rest of the year doesn't: a clean line between what happened and what's next. For optometry and dental practices, medical offices, service businesses, and churches across Bloomington and Indianapolis, the new year is when patient volume settles, holiday scheduling ends, and there's finally a window to look at the numbers instead of just running the business day to day.

That window closes fast. Practice owners who wait until March to reconcile last year's accounts usually end up rushing tax prep and missing planning opportunities they'd have caught in January. Accord Advisors works as an outsourced accounting department for a fixed monthly fee, so this checkpoint isn't something you're doing alone at your kitchen table with a shoebox of receipts.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: year-end-financial-review.jpg | alt: "Accountant and business owner reviewing year-end financial records together" | query: "accountant reviewing documents office" -->
## Year-end financial housekeeping for practices and businesses

Before you close the books on last year, a few things need attention regardless of whether you run an optometry practice, a service business, or a church office. Skipping this step is how February turns into a scramble.

- Reconcile every bank and credit card account through December 31, including any accounts opened or closed mid-year
- Confirm patient or client accounts receivable are accurate, especially for medical and optometry practices working through insurance reimbursements
- Verify payroll totals match W-2 and 1099 filings before those forms go out
- Reconcile fixed assets and equipment purchases, particularly for dental and optometry practices that update exam or lab equipment regularly
- Pull together year-end contribution statements and designated fund records for churches and religious organizations
- Review outstanding loans, lines of credit, and vendor balances so nothing carries an error into the new year

Handled inside our secure online platform, this housekeeping becomes a checklist you review with us, not a project you tackle solo after hours.

## Getting your books ready for tax season

Clean books make tax season shorter, cheaper, and a lot less stressful. When your accounts are reconciled and your income and expenses are categorized correctly before your CPA opens the return, there's no time lost chasing down missing receipts or untangling a mystery deposit from October.

This is where Outsourced Accounting and Personal Income Tax work together. If we've been closing your books monthly throughout the year, your business return and your personal return draw from the same accurate numbers, prepared by the same team that already knows your practice or business. Jared Hammack, CPA, and the Accord Advisors team handle both sides so nothing gets lost in translation between your bookkeeper and your tax preparer, because they're the same people.

For practice owners, this also means fewer surprises at filing time. You'll know roughly where you stand well before the deadline, instead of finding out in April.

## Setting a strong foundation for the year ahead

A new year is also a natural time to revisit the basics: entity structure, bookkeeping systems, and compliance requirements that may have been set up in a hurry when you first opened your doors. Optometry and dental practices, growing service businesses, and churches all reach a point where the original setup no longer fits the size or complexity of the operation.

Business Foundation Services exists for exactly this reset. Whether you're forming a new entity, correcting a chart of accounts that's grown messy, or putting real bookkeeping processes in place for the first time, this is the groundwork that makes every future tax season and financial decision easier. It's the difference between reacting to problems and having systems that catch them early.

Startups benefit here too. If you opened a practice or launched a service business in the last year, January is the right time to make sure the foundation matches where you're headed, not just where you started.

## Industry-specific priorities entering the new year

Different industries walk into January with different priorities. Nearly 40 years of serving healthcare providers has shown us what actually matters at this point in the calendar for each type of client.

### Optometry Practices
icon: ClipboardCheck

Review insurance panel changes, reconcile patient AR, and check that equipment purchases from last year are properly recorded before depreciation schedules run.

### Dentists
icon: HeartPulse

Confirm production and collection totals match, review hygiene department numbers, and make sure any associate compensation changes are reflected correctly going into the new year.

### Medical Professionals
icon: Stethoscope

Reconcile reimbursement adjustments from payers, verify provider compensation structures, and check that any new service lines added last year are set up correctly in the books.

### Service Businesses
icon: Wrench

Review job costing accuracy, reconcile equipment and vehicle purchases, and confirm subcontractor 1099 records are complete before filing deadlines.

### Churches
icon: Building2

Finalize contribution statements, reconcile designated and restricted funds separately, and confirm staff compensation and housing allowance records are documented correctly.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Entering the new year

**Q: Why is January a good time to review my practice's finances?**
A: January is a natural break between fiscal years, before new patient volume or projects ramp up. Reviewing reconciliations, payroll totals, and receivables now catches errors before they carry into tax filings, and sets up cleaner numbers for the months ahead.

**Q: What should optometry and dental practices reconcile before year end?**
A: Practices should reconcile patient accounts receivable, confirm equipment purchases are recorded correctly, and verify payroll matches W-2 and 1099 filings. Insurance panel or reimbursement changes from the prior year should also be reviewed before the new year's numbers start layering on top.

**Q: How does outsourced accounting help with new year tax preparation?**
A: When books are reconciled monthly throughout the year, tax preparation starts from accurate numbers instead of a year's worth of unsorted transactions. Accord Advisors' Outsourced Accounting and Personal Income Tax services work from the same records, reducing back-and-forth during filing season.

**Q: What are Business Foundation Services used for?**
A: Business Foundation Services help new or growing businesses, practices, and churches set up or correct entity structure, bookkeeping systems, and compliance basics. It's commonly used at startup or when an existing setup no longer matches the size of the operation.

**Q: Does Accord Advisors serve churches and nonprofits in Indiana?**
A: Yes. Churches and religious organizations in Bloomington and Indianapolis work with Accord Advisors on contribution statement accuracy, restricted fund reconciliation, and staff compensation records, alongside outsourced accounting support year-round.
