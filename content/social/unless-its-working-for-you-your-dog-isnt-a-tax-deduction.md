## LinkedIn

A golden retriever that greets patients in an optometry waiting room isn't a tax deduction, no matter how good the dog is at its job. That's the real question Bloomington and Indianapolis business owners ask every tax season, and the answer almost always comes down to Publication 535: a business expense has to be ordinary and necessary, not just nice to have. The exceptions are narrow but real. A trained guide or hearing dog can count as a medical expense under Publication 502, though only once costs clear 7.5% of adjusted gross income. A guard dog at a warehouse, a for-profit breeding kennel, a working farm dog, these can qualify as legitimate business expenses if the function is documented. Jared Hammack, CPA, walks Bloomington and Indianapolis clients through exactly this kind of judgment call before it becomes an audit problem.
