## LinkedIn

A Bloomington optometrist hadn't opened his portfolio statement in three years. He'd built it at 70% stocks, 30% bonds right after selling part of his practice. By the time he looked again, tech gains had pushed him to 85% stocks with almost no bond cushion left, and a market pullback cost him more than it should have.

Nobody decided to get more aggressive. The market decided for him, one unmonitored quarter at a time. That's the risk for medical professionals, dentists, and service business owners who set an allocation years ago and never revisited it against today's income, debt, or timeline to sell a practice.

Rebalancing also carries a tax bill if you're not careful. Jared Hammack, CPA, walks individual clients through coordinating rebalancing decisions with tax planning, not just at filing time in April.
